OUR PROCESS

Everything Begins Underground

Long before a beam holds a roof or a bar reinforces a wall, there is only rock and fire. Shakambhari chose not to buy steel, but to build the entire chain that makes it — our own mines, our own reduction, our own furnace, our own hands at every stage. What follows is that journey, six stages from raw earth to reinforced tomorrow. #SteelWithin isn't a line on a label; it's the discipline running through all six, so that whatever carries our name carries the strength of everything that came before it.

Slag Isn't Waste

Material tapped from the furnace splits two ways. The metal moves straight into cleaning, sizing, weighing and lot preparation for dispatch. The slag mixed with it goes through jigging instead of the bin — a process that separates by density, recovering additional metal back into the main stream and leaving behind a slag by-product that's put to use in road construction and land filling.

A Closed Cooling & Power Loop

The furnace transformer draws power from the power house and steps it up to run the furnace itself, while a cooling tower and pump house keep water circulating continuously through the furnace to manage the heat. Waste transformer oil is never discharged — it's drained and stored safely in drums.

PART 2 · THE FULL CHAIN

Circular Energy & Ferroalloy Ecosystem

Nothing here is wasted, and nothing here is idle capacity. The DRI process's own waste heat generates the power that runs the plant, while the same furnace that feeds our own steel also supplies alloys to producers well beyond it.

Machines & Divisions Materials & Products — hover over any box for details

Waste Heat → Power

The DRI process that converts pellets into sponge iron generates intense waste heat. Instead of letting it escape, the plant captures it and routes it to an internal power plant that generates electricity — self-produced power that runs the Steel Melting Shop, Rolling Mills, Ferroalloy Plant and the DRI Plant itself.

Silicomanganese & Ferromanganese

Silicomanganese is formulated in-house — a portion kept for captive consumption in the steel-making furnace, the surplus sold externally. Ferromanganese is also made in-house, but since it isn't required for our long-product focus, 100% of it is exported or sold to large flat-steel producers, foundries and automotive-grade steel manufacturers.

Shaped for Purpose

Pelletization — SIPL Rukni

Raw ore fines alone are unruly — too fine, too inconsistent for the kiln ahead. At SIPL Rukni they're agglomerated into uniform spherical pellets, engineered for the permeability that clean, efficient reduction demands.

CHAPTER III

The Stripping Away

Direct Reduced Iron — Rotary Kiln

At roughly 950°C, pellets and mined coal meet inside the rotary kiln. Oxygen is stripped away; only metallic iron remains. No coking, no blast furnace — impurities fall, and what's left is closer to the strength we're building toward.

CHAPTER IV

Where It Becomes One

Steel Making — Induction Furnace

Sponge iron meets externally sourced scrap and pig iron, and silicomanganese made entirely in-house — three sources, melted at roughly 1600°C into one liquid steel. Almost none of it was bought as a finished input we simply had to have; nearly all of it, we made ourselves.
