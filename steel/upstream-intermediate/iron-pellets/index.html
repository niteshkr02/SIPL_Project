SUB-PRODUCT 8.1

Iron Pellets

The uniform spheres that turn raw ore fines into something a kiln can actually reduce.

View Specifications Request Bulk Quote
WHAT IS IT?

Raw Ore, Made Kiln-Ready

Raw iron ore fines alone are too fine and inconsistent to feed a reduction kiln directly — they choke airflow and reduce unevenly. At our SIPL Rukni pellet plant, those fines are mixed with limestone, dolomite and bentonite binders, agglomerated into uniform spherical pellets, and thermally hardened.

The result is a spheroid engineered specifically for the permeability that clean, efficient reduction demands — the first manufactured step in a chain that ends in a finished steel bar.

HOW IT'S MADE

The Pelletisation Process

Eight stages between a pile of ore fines and a hardened, kiln-ready sphere — one continuous line, from grinding to screening.

Phase 1 — Raw Material Preparation
Iron Ore FinesLimestoneAnthraciteDolomite

Dryer

Raw iron ore fines and flux material arrive with excess moisture. The dryer removes it before grinding, so the mill isn't fighting wet, clumping ore.

Grinder

Dried ore and flux are ground down to the fine, consistent particle size that balling actually needs — too coarse and the green balls won't hold together.

BinderWater / Slurry

Mixer

Ground ore is blended with a binder and water (or slurry) until the mix is uniform and cohesive enough to roll into a ball that holds its shape.

Balling Equipment

The mixed feed is rolled into uniform "green balls" — soft, unfired spheres, sized precisely so every one fires evenly in the stages ahead.

Phase 2 — Induration (Thermal Hardening)

Travelling Grate

Green balls ride a moving grate through sequential drying and preheating zones, fed by hot air recirculated from the kiln and cooler through an air heater — building heat gradually so the balls don't crack.

Rotary Kiln

Preheated balls pass into the rotating kiln, where sustained high heat fires them into hard, dense, thermally hardened spheres — the defining step that gives pellets their strength.

Phase 3 — Cooling & Finishing

Annular Cooler

Fired pellets pass through multiple forced-air cooling zones before handling. The hot air pulled off them isn't wasted — it's routed back to the air heater to help fire the next batch.

Product Screen

Cooled pellets are screened to size — undersize material is recycled back into the process rather than shipped, so every finished pellet meets the same spec.

A closed-loop by design: hot air drawn off the rotary kiln and annular cooler is recirculated through the air heater back into the travelling grate, and undersize pellets from the product screen are recycled rather than discarded — the same discipline behind every stage of this line.

PRIMARY PURPOSE & BEST USE CASES

From Fines to Feedstock

Iron pellets exist to solve one problem: giving a reduction process a consistent, permeable charge to work with.

DRI Rotary Kilns

Feeds our own rotary kilns, where pellets and coal meet at roughly 950°C to strip oxygen and produce sponge iron — no coking, no blast furnace required.

Blast Furnace Operators

Equally suited as premium feedstock for external blast furnace operators who need a consistent, high-permeability charge at scale.

WHY CHOOSE OUR IRON PELLETS?

Engineered for the Kiln, Not Just Rounded Ore

Inconsistent ore fines choke a kiln's airflow and waste fuel on uneven reduction. Our pellets are engineered to a uniform size and hardness specifically for the permeability a reduction kiln needs — clean, efficient, predictable reduction, batch after batch.

Iron Pellets — Common Questions

What size are your iron pellets?

Uniform 6–16mm spheroids, sized specifically for consistent kiln permeability.

What binders are used in production?

Iron ore fines are agglomerated with limestone, dolomite and bentonite binders before thermal hardening.

Do you supply pellets outside your own kilns?

Yes — SIPL Rukni produces at 0.9+ MTPA scale, enough to feed our own rotary kilns and supply external DRI and blast furnace operators.
